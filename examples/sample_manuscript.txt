Attention-Guided Sparse Routing for Efficient Multilingual Translation

Jane Q. Researcher, Alan Example, and Maria Sample
Department of Computer Science, Example University
jane.researcher@example.edu, alan@example.edu

Abstract
We propose Attention-Guided Sparse Routing (AGSR), a mixture-of-experts layer
that routes tokens to experts using a lightweight attention signal rather than a
learned gating network. On three multilingual translation benchmarks, AGSR
matches dense-model BLEU while reducing inference FLOPs by 41%. We further show
that attention-based routing yields more balanced expert utilization than
top-k gating, mitigating the expert-collapse problem. Our method requires no
auxiliary load-balancing loss.

1 Introduction
Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models scale capacity without proportional compute, but
their learned gating networks are unstable and often collapse onto a few experts.
In this work we ask whether the attention distribution already present in a
transformer can serve as a routing signal. We introduce AGSR and evaluate it on
multilingual machine translation.

2 Method
AGSR computes a routing distribution from the query-key attention scores of the
preceding self-attention layer, then dispatches each token to the top-2 experts.
Because the signal is derived from attention, no additional gating parameters are
introduced. We describe the dispatch and combine operations and their complexity.

3 Experiments
We evaluate on WMT14 En-De, WMT17 En-Zh, and a 12-language low-resource set. We
compare against a dense transformer and a standard top-2 MoE with a load-balancing
loss. AGSR matches dense BLEU within 0.3 points while using 41% fewer inference
FLOPs. Expert utilization entropy is higher than the top-2 baseline.

4 Limitations
We evaluate only on translation and do not test on larger model scales. The
compute savings assume an idealized dispatch with no communication overhead.

5 Conclusion
AGSR shows that attention can double as a routing signal, removing gating
parameters and the load-balancing loss while preserving quality.
